Paso 2 de 8

Fíjate Notice

Read how it works. Play the examples and repeat them aloud.

What time is it? ¿Qué hora es?

For one o'clock use es la; for all the others son las: Es la una. Son las tres. Add minutes with y: y cinco, y diez, y cuarto (quarter past), y media (half past). For the minutes before the hour, Mexicans usually say cuarto para las… or diez para las…: Son diez para las ocho (7:50). You'll also hear las ocho menos diez. Add de la mañana (a.m.), de la tarde (afternoon, until about 7 p.m.), de la noche.

clockSpanish
1:00Es la una.
3:10Son las tres y diez.
4:15Son las cuatro y cuarto.
6:30Son las seis y media.
7:45Es cuarto para las ocho. / Son las ocho menos cuarto.
11:20 p.m.Son las once y veinte de la noche.

From the conversation:

  • Son las once y veinte.It's twenty past eleven.
  • Es la una y media.It's half past one.
  • Son diez para las ocho de la noche.It's ten to eight in the evening.

Schedules, tickets and phones use the 24-hour clock (17:30), but in conversation people say las cinco y media de la tarde.

At what time? From… to…

¿A qué hora…? asks when something happens; answer a la una, a las cuatro y cuarto. Opening hours: de … a …: de nueve a siete (from nine to seven), de lunes a viernes.

From the conversation:

  • Sí, el martes está bien. ¿A qué hora?Yes, Tuesday's fine. At what time?
  • A las diez y media de la mañana o a las cuatro y cuarto de la tarde.At ten thirty in the morning or a quarter past four in the afternoon.
  • Sí, los sábados de nueve a dos. De lunes a viernes, de nueve a siete.Yes, on Saturdays from nine to two. Monday to Friday, from nine to seven.

Days and dates

Days and months are written with a small letter: lunes, martes, octubre. El lunes = on Monday (this Monday); los lunes = on Mondays (every Monday). Dates go day, de, month: el trece de octubre. For the first of the month, Mexicans say el primero: el primero de enero. To ask the date: ¿Qué fecha es hoy? / ¿Qué día es hoy?

Cultura Culture

Read about the culture behind the language.

Dates to remember, and ahorita

Dates to know: 16 September, Independence Day, is an official rest day (día de descanso obligatorio). It is celebrated on the night of the 15th with el Grito, when the president rings a bell and shouts ¡Viva México! from the National Palace. The other official rest days under the Federal Labor Law (art. 74) are 1 January, the first Monday of February, the third Monday of March, 1 May, the third Monday of November, 25 December and, every six years, 1 October (when a new president takes office).

Some of the best-loved dates are traditional celebrations, not official rest days, so most people still work: 1 and 2 November, Día de Muertos, when families build altars for their dead; 12 December, the Virgin of Guadalupe; and the posadas, Christmas parties from 16 to 24 December.

Ahorita literally means "right now", but it can mean "in a while" or even "maybe never": ahorita lo hago might be in five minutes or this evening. Doctors' and work appointments are kept on time; for a party at a friend's house, arriving 20–30 minutes after the time on the invitation is normal.

Since October 2022 most of Mexico no longer changes the clocks: daylight saving time was abolished, except in some municipalities on the US border.

Elsewhere: In Spain, people say las ocho menos cuarto rather than cuarto para las ocho, and ahora simply means "now".

Sources

  • Diario Oficial de la Federación, Ley de los Husos Horarios en los Estados Unidos Mexicanos (28 October 2022).
  • Ley Federal del Trabajo, art. 74 (official rest days), Cámara de Diputados, current text.

Role play: Book an appointment by phone

You phone a hair salon (or a dentist) in Mexico City to make an appointment. You work Monday to Friday until 5 p.m. Use usted.

You

  1. Greet the receptionist and say you'd like an appointment.
  2. Suggest a day; ask what times there are.
  3. Choose a time that fits your work.
  4. Give your name.
  5. Ask the opening hours on Saturday and say goodbye (¡Hasta el…!).

La recepcionista

  1. Answers the phone.
  2. Offers a day and two times.
  3. Asks your name.
  4. Repeats the day, date and time.
  5. Gives the Saturday hours.
