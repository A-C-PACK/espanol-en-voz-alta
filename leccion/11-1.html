Fíjate Notice

A review of the A2 grammar. Play the examples; check anything you've forgotten.

Telling a trip: preterite or imperfect?

The imperfect sets the scene: what a place was like (era precioso), the weather (hacía mucho sol), what there was or wasn't (no había agua caliente) and what you wanted (quería bañarme). The preterite tells the events, one after another: llegué, fui, llamé, me cambiaron, comí. To sum up how something was, use the preterite of ser: la callejoneada fue increíble.

imperfect: the scenepreterite: the events
El hotel era una casa antigua.Llegué el viernes trece en la tarde.
Hacía mucho sol.El sábado fui a Monte Albán.
Quería bañarme, pero no había agua caliente.Entonces llamé a la recepción.
Casi no picaba.El domingo comí mole en el mercado.

From the conversation:

  • Llegué el viernes trece en la tarde. El hotel era una casa antigua, con un patio y muchas plantas. ¡Era precioso!I arrived on Friday the thirteenth in the afternoon. The hotel was an old house, with a courtyard and lots of plants. It was gorgeous!
  • El sábado fui a Monte Albán muy temprano. Hacía mucho sol, pero la vista desde arriba era increíble.On Saturday I went to Monte Albán very early. It was very sunny, but the view from the top was amazing.
  • Bueno, uno pequeño. El sábado en la mañana quería bañarme, pero no había agua caliente. Entonces llamé a la recepción y me cambiaron de habitación. ¡La nueva era más grande que la primera!Well, a small one. On Saturday morning I wanted to take a shower, but there was no hot water. So I called reception and they moved me to another room. The new one was bigger than the first!

Explaining a problem uses both: Anoche funcionó bien y hoy ya no (events), no había agua caliente (the situation).

Object pronouns: me, te, le · lo, la, los, las

Me, te, le say *to* or *for* whom: ¿qué me recomienda?, los dos me encantaron, le hablo de la habitación ocho (I'm calling you, usted). Lo, la, los, las replace a thing and agree with it: la dejé correr (el agua is feminine), lo tomé de agua (el chocolate). Both go before the conjugated verb. Together, the person comes first: ¿me lo sirve? When you hear se lo traigo, se stands for le (to you, to him, to her).

the thingwith a pronoun
¿Me sirve el mole con arroz?¿Me lo sirve con arroz?
Dejé correr el agua.La dejé correr.
Compré los boletos ayer.Los compré ayer.
¿Me cambia la chamarra?¿Me la cambia?

Comparing: más … que, tan … como, mejor

más / menos + adjective + que · tan + adjective + como · tanto, tanta + noun + como

Más / menos … que: más grande que la primera. With a noun it works the same way: más fotos de comida que de iglesias. Tan … como with an adjective, tanto / tanta … como with a noun or after a verb: no hace tanto frío. Four one-word comparatives: mejor, peor, mayor, menor: la comida es mucho mejor (never más buena).

  • Claro que sí. Tengo libre la habitación cinco, en la planta baja. Es más grande que la suya y tiene vista al patio.Of course. Room five is free, on the ground floor. It's bigger than yours and it looks onto the courtyard.
  • Oaxaca. ¡Tengo más fotos de comida que de iglesias!Oaxaca. I have more photos of food than of churches!

Plans: ir a, pensar, the future and si

Three ways to talk about what's next. Ir a + infinitivo, the everyday one: voy a ir a Puerto Escondido. Pensar + infinitivo for a plan or an intention: pienso regresar a Oaxaca. The future for promises, predictions and plans that depend on something: si tengo dinero, me quedaré una semana (si + present, future). A fixed plan can also go in the present: a las ocho y media salgo para Monte Albán.

From the conversation:

  • En Semana Santa voy a ir a Puerto Escondido con Diego: esta vez él escoge. Y en julio pienso regresar a Oaxaca para ver la Guelaguetza. Si tengo dinero, me quedaré una semana.At Easter I'm going to go to Puerto Escondido with Diego: this time he chooses. And in July I'm planning to go back to Oaxaca to see the Guelaguetza. If I have the money, I'll stay a week.
  • Gracias, pero es que a las ocho y media salgo para Monte Albán. ¿Me podría cambiar de habitación?Thanks, but the thing is, at half past eight I'm leaving for Monte Albán. Could you move me to another room?

When si means "if" (a condition), never use the future right after it: si tengo dinero, not si tendré dinero.

Directions: siga, cruce, tome

To give directions to a stranger, use the usted command: siga derecho (go straight on), cruce (cross), tome (take), dé vuelta a la derecha (turn right), salga (go out). With a friend, use the tú command, which looks like the present of él / ella: sigue, cruza, toma, da vuelta. Hay que + infinitivo says what anyone has to do: ¿hay que dar vuelta?

Dé (usted) has a written accent; da (tú) doesn't.

Oaxaca: stones, mole and the Guelaguetza

Oaxaca de Juárez, capital of the southern state of Oaxaca, lies in a high valley at about 1,550 meters, some six to seven hours by bus from Mexico City. Many of its colonial buildings are made of a pale green stone (cantera verde). The heart of the city is the zócalo, a square shaded by big laurel trees, with a bandstand in the middle and cafés under the arcades. Near the cathedral, the pedestrian street Macedonio Alcalá (the andador) leads north to Santo Domingo, a baroque church whose former monastery now houses the Museo de las Culturas de Oaxaca.

On a flattened mountaintop about 10 km west of the city stands Monte Albán. It was founded around 500 BC and was the capital of the Zapotecs for more than a thousand years: it has great plazas, pyramids, a ball court and the carved stones known as Los Danzantes. Together, the Historic Center of Oaxaca and Monte Albán have been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1987.

Oaxaca is famous as the land of the seven moles; the list usually includes negro, rojo, coloradito, amarillo, verde, chichilo and manchamanteles, though cooks don't all agree. Mole negro, the darkest, combines several chiles with chocolate, spices and seeds, and is usually not very hot. A tlayuda is a big, thin, crisp tortilla spread with asiento (pork lard) and beans, topped with quesillo (Oaxaca's stringy cheese) and meat, and toasted over coals: a favorite at night. And Oaxacans drink chocolate: cacao ground with sugar, cinnamon and often almonds, beaten until it foams and served de agua or de leche, with pan de yema for dipping.

In July, the Guelaguetza brings dance groups from the state's eight regions to the open-air auditorium on the Cerro del Fortín (see lesson 10.3). The main shows are on the Lunes del Cerro, usually the two Mondays after 16 July; in 2027 that would be 19 and 26 July, but check the official program, because the dates can change.

Elsewhere: Hot chocolate is popular across the Spanish-speaking world. In Spain, chocolate con churros is thick hot chocolate for dipping fried dough. In Bogotá, chocolate santafereño comes with cheese, which people drop into the cup to melt.

Role play: My best trip, in two minutes

It's your turn at the language exchange: tell the group about your best trip in 1–2 minutes. Then Mariana asks you a few questions. Record yourself if you can.

You

  1. Say where and when you went, how you traveled and with whom.
  2. Describe the place and the weather with the imperfect (era, había, hacía).
  3. Say what you did, in the preterite, with connectors (primero, después, el sábado…).
  4. Tell one problem or surprise (un día, de repente, entonces, al final).
  5. Compare it with another place, and finish with a plan (pienso…, si…).

Mariana

  1. Invites you to start.
  2. Asks what the place was like.
  3. Asks what you did.
  4. Asks if anything went wrong.
  5. Asks you to compare it with another trip.
