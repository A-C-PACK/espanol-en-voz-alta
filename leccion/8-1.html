Fíjate Notice

Read how it works. Play the examples and repeat them aloud.

Plans: ir a + infinitivo

voy · vas · va · vamos · van + a + infinitivo

You met voy a… in 4.2. Here is the whole structure: the present of ir + a + any infinitive. It's the most common way to talk about plans in Mexico. Add a time: hoy en la noche, mañana, el sábado, el fin de semana, la próxima semana. Vamos a + infinitivo also means "let's…": vamos a comer tostadas.

ira + infinitivo
yovoya correr
túvasa hacer
él, ella, ustedvaa venir
nosotros, nosotrasvamosa comer
ellos, ellas, ustedesvana llevar máscaras

From the conversation:

  • Oye, Alex, ¿qué vas a hacer el sábado?Hey, Alex, what are you going to do on Saturday?
  • En la mañana voy a correr, como siempre. En la tarde no tengo planes. ¿Por qué?In the morning I'm going to run, as usual. In the afternoon I don't have any plans. Why?
  • Sí, mejor, porque siempre hay fila. Ahorita compro los boletos. ¡Ah! Y mi hermana Lucía va a venir también.Yes, better, because there's always a line. I'll buy the tickets right now. Oh! And my sister Lucía is going to come too.

Don't forget the a: voy a correr, not voy correr.

Suggesting: ¿quieres…?, ¿te late…?, ¿por qué no…?

Three ways to suggest a plan to a friend, all + infinitive or a verb: ¿Quieres + infinitivo? (do you want to…?), neutral and safe anywhere. ¿Te late + infinitivo? (do you want to…? / does … sound good?) is very Mexican and informal: it comes from latir (to beat, like a heart), so it's "does your heart beat for it?". Use it with friends only, never with usted. You can answer ¡Me late! (I like that idea!). ¿Por qué no + nosotros form? (why don't we…?) suggests something different: ¿por qué no vamos a las tres?

suggestionmeaning
¿Quieres ir al museo?Do you want to go to the museum?
¿Te late ir a bailar?Do you want to go dancing? (friends, Mexico)
¿Por qué no vamos a las tres?Why don't we go at three?
Vamos a comer tostadas.Let's eat tostadas.

  • Es que tengo ganas de salir. Mira, aquí en el celular tengo unas ideas. ¿Te late ir a bailar salsa en la noche?Well, I feel like going out. Look, I've got a few ideas here on my phone. Do you want to go salsa dancing in the evening?
  • ¡Ay, Alex! Bueno, ¿y quieres ir al Museo Frida Kahlo? Está aquí en Coyoacán, cerca de mi casa.Oh, Alex! OK, so do you want to go to the Frida Kahlo Museum? It's here in Coyoacán, near my place.
  • A las doce no puedo: tengo que ayudar a doña Lupita en la casa. ¿Por qué no vamos a las tres?I can't at twelve: I have to help Doña Lupita at home. Why don't we go at three?

Yes, no, and tener ganas de…

tengo · tienes · tiene · tenemos · tienen + ganas de + infinitivo

Tener ganas de + infinitivo (an Andamio structure) = to feel like doing something, to really want to: tengo ganas de salir. Add muchas for "really": tengo muchas ganas de conocer la Casa Azul. To say yes among friends: ¡Sí, claro!, ¡Claro que sí! and three very Mexican words for "OK! / Deal!": ¡Va!, ¡Sale!, ¡Órale! To say no softly, start with Mmm… la verdad… (to be honest…), give a reason (no puedo, tengo que…) and, if you can, suggest something else.

From the conversation:

  • Mmm… la verdad, no tengo muchas ganas de bailar. ¡Bailo muy mal!Mmm… to be honest, I don't really feel like dancing. I dance really badly!
  • ¡Eso sí! Tengo muchas ganas de conocer la Casa Azul.Now that, yes! I really want to see the Blue House.
  • Va, a las tres. Y después vamos a comer tostadas al mercado de Coyoacán, ¿no? ¡Son buenísimas!OK, at three. And afterwards we'll go and eat tostadas at the Coyoacán market, right? They're amazing!

Conocer a place = to see it or visit it for the first time: quiero conocer Oaxaca (I want to go to Oaxaca, I've never been).

Time and place: quedar

To fix the details, ask ¿A qué hora quedamos? (what time shall we meet?) and ¿Dónde quedamos? (where shall we meet?). Here quedar means "to arrange to meet"; it's a different meaning from ¿Cómo me queda? (how does it fit me?) in 7.3. Answer with a time (a las tres, a cuarto para las tres) and a place (en la entrada del museo, en el metro Cuauhtémoc). Close the plan with Entonces nos vemos… + day + time.

From the conversation:

  • ¡Órale! ¿Y dónde quedamos?Great! So where shall we meet?
  • En la entrada del museo, en la calle Londres. Es una casa azul: no hay pierde.At the entrance to the museum, on Londres street. It's a blue house: you can't miss it.
  • ¿Y a qué hora quedamos? ¿A cuarto para las tres?And what time shall we meet? At a quarter to three?
  • ¡Qué padre! Entonces nos vemos el sábado a cuarto para las tres.Cool! So see you on Saturday at a quarter to three.

You'll also hear ¿Dónde nos vemos? (same meaning) and, in Mexico, quedamos de vernos a las tres (we agreed to meet at three).

Cultura Culture

Read about the culture behind the language.

On time, or a little late? And what to do on the weekend

Mexicans are on time for work, classes, the doctor, business meetings and anything with a fixed start: a bus, a movie, a museum ticket with an entry time. Social invitations are different. For a party or a dinner at someone's home, arriving at exactly the time on the invitation can be a little awkward (the hosts may still be cooking); arriving twenty to forty minutes later is normal in many circles, and nobody needs to apologize. For a meeting in a public place (quedamos a las tres en el metro), be on time, or send a message: voy tarde, llego en diez minutos. When in doubt, ask: ¿Es a las ocho en punto? People joke about la hora mexicana, but in big-city work life punctuality is expected.

Weekend plans in Mexico City. On Sundays, the Muévete en Bici ride closes Paseo de la Reforma to cars. In the south, families rent trajineras on the canals of Xochimilco. In Coyoacán, the Museo Frida Kahlo (the Casa Azul, calle Londres 247) is open Tuesday to Sunday; weekend tickets often sell out, so people buy them online in advance. And there is lucha libre: masked wrestlers, good guys (técnicos) against bad guys (rudos), and a loud, family crowd. The Arena México in the colonia Doctores, opened in 1956, is called the "cathedral" of lucha libre; its company, the CMLL, usually has shows on Tuesday and Friday nights and Sunday afternoons. In 2018 Mexico City declared lucha libre part of its intangible cultural heritage.

Elsewhere: In Spain, people make plans with ¿quedamos? and ¿te apetece…? (instead of ¿te late…?) and answer ¡vale! (instead of ¡va! or ¡sale!). In Argentina you'll hear ¿te copa…?

Role play: Plans for Saturday

Your friend Diego calls you to make plans for Saturday. You have a commitment on Saturday morning. Use tú.

You

  1. Say what you're going to do on Saturday morning.
  2. Decline one of his ideas politely, with a reason.
  3. Accept another idea, or suggest your own (¿por qué no…?).
  4. Ask where and what time you're meeting.
  5. Close the plan: Entonces nos vemos…

  1. Asks what you're going to do on Saturday.
  2. Suggests playing soccer at 10 a.m.
