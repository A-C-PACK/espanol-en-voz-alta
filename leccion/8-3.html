Fíjate Notice

Read how it works. Play the examples and repeat them aloud.

Polite requests: ¿Me podría…? and quisiera…

With hotel staff, use usted and two very polite fixed phrases. ¿Me podría + infinitivo? = Could you … (for me)? It's softer than ¿me puede…?. Quisiera + noun / infinitive = I would like (you met it in 3.2). They are forms of poder and querer that you'll study at B1; for now, learn them as chunks. You'll hear staff say ¿Me permite…? (May I have / see…?) and con mucho gusto (with pleasure). Thank them with muy amable (very kind of you).

polite phrasemeaning
Quisiera hacer una reservación.I'd like to make a reservation.
¿Me podría dar la contraseña del wifi?Could you give me the wifi password?
¿Me podría mandar la confirmación?Could you send me the confirmation?
¿Me permite una identificación?May I see some ID?

From the conversation:

  • Buenos días. Quisiera hacer una reservación, por favor.Good morning. I'd like to make a reservation, please.
  • Muy amable. ¿Me podría dar la contraseña del wifi?That's very kind. Could you give me the wifi password?
  • Gracias. Quisiera ir a Monte Albán mañana. ¿Me podría recomendar un tour?Thank you. I'd like to go to Monte Albán tomorrow. Could you recommend a tour?

Dates and length of stay

From one date to another: del … al …: del trece al dieciséis de noviembre (de + el = del, a + el = al). Say the month only once, at the end. Count nights: tres noches; prices are por noche (per night). Times at a hotel: la hora de entrada (check-in) a partir de las tres (from three on) and la hora de salida (check-out). In Mexico, a las doce del día = at twelve noon.

The room: sencilla, doble; está incluido

A habitación sencilla is for one person; a habitación doble is for two (with one big bed, una cama matrimonial, or two beds). Ask what the price includes with ¿… está incluido? The participle agrees like an adjective: el desayuno está incluido, la comida no está incluida, los impuestos están incluidos (taxes are included). Ask about the room with ¿Tiene…?: ¿tiene aire acondicionado / ventilador / baño propio?

From the conversation:

  • Claro, aquí está mi pasaporte. Disculpe, ¿el desayuno está incluido?Of course, here's my passport. Excuse me, is breakfast included?
  • Sí, señor, está incluido. Es de siete y media a diez y media, aquí en el patio.Yes, sir, it's included. It's from seven thirty to ten thirty, here in the courtyard.
  • ¡Qué bien! ¿Y la habitación tiene aire acondicionado?Great! And does the room have air conditioning?

Mexican words: la reservación (not la reserva), la habitación or el cuarto at a hotel, but la recámara for a bedroom at home. Upstairs is la planta alta; the ground floor, la planta baja.

Cultura Culture

Read about the culture behind the language.

Puentes and colonial hotels

A puente ("bridge") is a long weekend. Since a 2006 reform of the Ley Federal del Trabajo (article 74), three national holidays are always on a Monday: the first Monday of February (Constitution Day, 5 February), the third Monday of March (Benito Juárez's birthday, 21 March) and the third Monday of November (the Revolution, 20 November). In 2026 they fall on 2 February, 16 March and 16 November. On these weekends, and at Easter (Semana Santa) and Christmas, buses and hotels fill up fast, so people book early. Schools sometimes add extra days off (check the SEP school calendar).

Favorite trips from Mexico City include Oaxaca (about six to seven hours by bus), Guanajuato and San Miguel de Allende. The Historic Center of Oaxaca and the Zapotec city of Monte Albán are a UNESCO World Heritage Site (1987), and so are Guanajuato (1988) and San Miguel de Allende (2008). In these colonial cities many small hotels are old houses (casonas) with rooms around a courtyard (patio) with plants and a fountain. Oaxaca is about 1,550 meters above sea level, so its nights are cool and many hotels have fans, not air conditioning.

Words: in Mexico you make a reservación; the room at a hotel is a habitación or cuarto, but a bedroom at home is a recámara.

Elsewhere: In Spain, people say la reserva (not reservación), habitación is also the bedroom at home, and an apartment is a piso. In Spain a long weekend is also a puente.

Sources

  • Diario Oficial de la Federación, 17 January 2006: reform of article 74 of the Ley Federal del Trabajo (Monday holidays).
  • UNESCO World Heritage List: Historic Centre of Oaxaca and Archaeological Site of Monte Albán (1987); Historic Town of Guanajuato and Adjacent Mines (1988); Protective town of San Miguel and the Sanctuary of Jesús Nazareno de Atotonilco (2008).

Role play: Checking in

You arrive at a small hotel in Guanajuato on Friday evening. You booked a single room for two nights. Use usted.

You

  1. Greet the receptionist and say you have a reservation in your name.
  2. Confirm the dates and the number of nights.
  3. Ask if breakfast is included and at what time.
  4. Ask what time check-out is.
  5. Make a polite request (¿Me podría…?) and thank him.

El recepcionista

  1. Welcomes you and asks how he can help.
  2. Finds the booking and checks the dates.
  3. Asks for ID.
  4. Answers your questions.
  5. Gives you the key and the room number.
