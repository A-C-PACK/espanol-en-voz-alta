The preterite: regular verbs

To talk about finished actions in the past (ayer, el sábado, anoche), Mexican Spanish uses the preterite (pretérito). Take off -ar, -er, -ir and add the endings below. -er and -ir verbs share the same endings. The yo and él / ella / usted forms carry a written accent: hablé, habló; comí, comió. Without it, hablo means "I speak", habló means "he spoke".

From the conversation:

  • Sí, Mike llegó el viernes en la noche. Su vuelo a Chicago salió hoy a las seis y media.Yes, Mike arrived on Friday night. His flight to Chicago left today at six thirty.
  • El domingo descansamos en la mañana y en la tarde comimos en la fonda de don Chuy. ¿Y tú qué hiciste?On Sunday we rested in the morning and in the afternoon we ate at Don Chuy's fonda. And what did you do?
  • ¡Estuvo buenísima! Mateo gritó toda la función y al final le compré una máscara de luchador.It was great! Mateo shouted through the whole show, and at the end I bought him a wrestler's mask.

Spelling changes in the yo form only, to keep the sound: llegar → llegué, jugar → jugué, pagar → pagué, buscar → busqué, sacar → saqué, empezar → empecé. And leer: leí, but él leyó, ellos leyeron. The nosotros form of -ar and -ir verbs is the same as in the present (caminamos, vivimos): the time expression tells you which one it is.

Six very common irregular verbs

These verbs have their own stems and no written accents: fui, hice, tuve, estuve, vi, di. Ir and ser share the same forms: fui al cine (I went), fue muy divertido (it was). Ver: vi, viste, vio, vimos, vieron. Dar: di, diste, dio, dimos, dieron. Note hizo with z. Two useful chunks: ¿Qué tal estuvo? (How was it?) and hizo sol / hizo frío (it was sunny / cold).

When? Past time expressions

Ayer (yesterday), anoche (last night), anteayer (the day before yesterday), el sábado (on Saturday, the last one), el sábado pasado, la semana pasada, el fin de semana pasado, hoy temprano (early today). Hace + time = ago: hace dos días (two days ago), hace una semana. In Mexico, en la mañana / en la tarde / en la noche also work for the past: el viernes en la noche.

From the conversation:

  • ¡Muy bien! En la mañana fui en bici por Reforma con mi amiga Sofía, del Ángel hasta el Zócalo. Después desayunamos tamales en el centro.Really well! In the morning I rode my bike along Reforma with my friend Sofía, from the Angel to the Zócalo. Then we had tamales for breakfast downtown.
  • ¡Qué energía! Yo anoche me dormí a las nueve.What energy! Last night I fell asleep at nine.

El sábado in a past sentence means last Saturday; los sábados means every Saturday (with the present): El sábado fui al cine vs. Los sábados voy al mercado.

Just done it: acabar de + infinitivo

A new Andamio structure. Acabar de in the present + an infinitive = to have just done something: acabo de llegar (I've just arrived), acabamos de comer (we've just eaten). It's in the present tense, even though it talks about the very recent past. To ask about someone's weekend, Mexicans often say ¿Cómo te fue? (How did it go?) → Me fue muy bien.

From the conversation:

Cultura Culture

Read about the culture behind the language.

A weekend in Mexico City: masks and pyramids

Lucha libre is Mexican professional wrestling: fast, acrobatic, and full of color. The wrestlers (luchadores) are either técnicos (the good guys, who follow the rules) or rudos (the bad guys), and many of the most famous wear a máscara all their lives; losing it in a lucha de apuestas is a big event. The Arena México, in the colonia Doctores, opened in 1956 and is known as the "cathedral" of lucha libre. It is the home of the CMLL (Consejo Mundial de Lucha Libre), founded in 1933 and considered the oldest wrestling promotion still active. Regular shows are usually on Tuesday and Friday evenings and Sunday afternoons (the family show), but times change, so check the CMLL's official pages before you go. In 2018 Mexico City declared lucha libre part of its intangible cultural heritage.

Teotihuacan, about 50 km northeast of the city, was one of the largest cities of the ancient Americas, at its height in roughly the 1st to 6th centuries AD. Its main avenue, the Calzada de los Muertos (Avenue of the Dead), connects the Pyramid of the Moon, the huge Pyramid of the Sun and the Temple of the Feathered Serpent. The Aztecs found it already in ruins and gave it its name, usually translated as "the place where the gods were created". It is an easy day trip: frequent buses leave the Terminal del Norte and take around an hour. Take a hat, sunscreen and water. The rules for climbing the pyramids have changed several times in recent years; check INAH's current visitor information.

And on Sunday mornings, many people do what Mariana does: cycle or walk along the Paseo de la Reforma during Muévete en Bici (see lesson 4.2).

Elsewhere: Around the Spanish-speaking world, the weekend question is the same, ¿Qué hiciste el fin de semana?, but in Spain people often use a different past tense for recent events: ¿Qué has hecho este fin de semana? You'll learn that tense at B1.

  • Consejo Mundial de Lucha Libre (CMLL), official site and weekly listings for the Arena México (Tuesday, Friday and Sunday shows; checked October 2026).
  • Gaceta Oficial de la Ciudad de México, 23 July 2018: declaration of lucha libre as intangible cultural heritage of Mexico City.
  • UNESCO World Heritage List: “Pre-Hispanic City of Teotihuacan” (inscribed 1987); INAH, Zona Arqueológica de Teotihuacan, visitor information.

Role play: Monday morning

It's Monday morning. A friend asks about your weekend. Tell them what you did on Saturday and Sunday, with whom and how it was, then ask about their weekend and react. Use tú.

You

  1. Answer the greeting and say how you are (use acabar de… if you can).
  2. Say what you did on Saturday: where, with whom, what you saw.
  3. Say what you did on Sunday.
  4. Say how it was (¡Estuvo…!, ¡Me encantó!).
  5. Ask your friend what they did and react (¿Neta?, ¡Qué divertido!, ¿Qué tal estuvo?).

Rocío

  1. Asks: ¿Qué tal tu fin de semana?
  2. Asks follow-up questions: ¿Con quién? ¿Y qué tal estuvo?
  3. Tells you she went to the lucha libre with her children.
  4. Says she has a class and has to go.
