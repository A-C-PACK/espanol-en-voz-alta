Paso 2 de 8

Fíjate Notice

Read how it works. Play the examples and repeat them aloud.

What's wrong with it?

In Mexico the most common phrase is no sirve: it doesn't work, it's no good. Also: no funciona (it doesn't work), no prende (it won't turn on; prender = to turn on), está descompuesto / descompuesta (it's broken down: machines, cars, phones), está roto / rota (it's broken: cracked, torn, in pieces). Descompuesto and roto agree with the thing: la licuadora está descompuesta, el vaso está roto. For the wrong item: no es para mi celular, no es mi talla, no es el color que pedí.

phraseuse it for
no sirve / no funcionaanything that doesn't work
no prendeit won't turn on
está descompuesto, -aa machine that has stopped working
está roto, -aphysically broken: cracked, torn
no es para…the wrong item

From the conversation:

  • Buenas tardes. Ayer compré esta licuadora aquí, pero no sirve.Good afternoon. I bought this blender here yesterday, but it doesn't work.
  • A ver… Sí, tiene razón, no prende. Está descompuesta. ¿Trae su ticket?Let's see… Yes, you're right, it won't turn on. It's broken. Do you have your receipt?
  • Ah, y otra cosa. Ayer también compré este cargador, pero no es para mi celular.Oh, and another thing. Yesterday I also bought this charger, but it isn't for my phone.

Tell the story with the preterite

To explain a problem, say when you bought it and what happened, in the preterite: Lo compré ayer, Ayer funcionó bien, Hoy ya no prendió. Ya no = not anymore. The salesclerk will often ask you questions in the preterite with usted: ¿Lo usó? (Did you use it?), ¿Pagó con tarjeta? (Did you pay by card?).

From the conversation:

  • Ayer funcionó bien: hice un licuado de plátano. Pero hoy en la mañana ya no prendió.Yesterday it worked fine: I made a banana smoothie. But this morning it didn't turn on anymore.
  • Claro que sí. ¿Pagó en efectivo o con tarjeta?Of course. Did you pay in cash or by card?
  • En efectivo. Fueron ciento ochenta pesos.In cash. It was a hundred and eighty pesos.

Accidents happen: se me olvidó, se me cayó

Spanish often describes accidents as if they happened by themselves, "to me". Learn these as fixed chunks: se me olvidó… (I forgot…), se me cayó… (I dropped…), se me rompió… (… broke on me). The verb agrees with the thing: se me olvidó el ticket, but se me olvidaron las llaves. To ask someone else with usted: ¿Se le cayó? You'll learn the full system at B1.

one thingmore than one
se me olvidó el ticketse me olvidaron las llaves
se me cayó el celularse me cayeron los lentes
se me rompió el vasose me rompieron los audífonos

From the conversation:

  • Mmm. ¿No se le cayó?Hmm. You didn't drop it?
  • No, no se me cayó. La lavé y la guardé, nada más.No, I didn't drop it. I washed it and put it away, that's all.
  • Sí, aquí está. Pero se me olvidó la caja en la casa. ¿Es un problema?Yes, here it is. But I forgot the box at home. Is that a problem?

Asking for a fix: ¿Me lo puede cambiar?

In unit 7 you learned lo, la, los, las (it, them). Put me first: ¿Me lo puede cambiar? (Can you change it for me?). Choose lo / la / los / las to match the thing: el cargador → me lo, la licuadora → me la, los audífonos → me los. To ask for a refund: ¿Me devuelve el dinero? (Will you give me my money back?). When the assistant answers, le becomes se before lo / la: se lo cambio (I'll change it for you).

the thingthe question
el cargador¿Me lo puede cambiar?
la licuadora¿Me la puede cambiar?
los audífonos¿Me los puede cambiar?
el dinero¿Me devuelve el dinero?

From the conversation:

  • ¿Me la puede cambiar por otra igual, por favor? Me gusta mucho este modelo.Can you exchange it for another one the same, please? I really like this model.
  • No, no lo usé. ¿Me lo puede cambiar?No, I didn't use it. Can you exchange it for me?
  • Entonces, ¿me devuelve el dinero, por favor?Then can you give me my money back, please?

Devolver changes o → ue in the present: ¿me devuelve…?, devuelvo. In the preterite it's regular: devolví, devolvió.

Cultura Culture

Read about the culture behind the language.

No sirve, and who can help: PROFECO

In Mexico, no sirve is the everyday way to say that something doesn't work: el elevador no sirve, mi celular ya no sirve. You'll also see handwritten signs that say just NO SIRVE on a broken machine. Broken machines are descompuestos; physically broken things are rotos. And the receipt is almost always el ticket.

PROFECO, the Procuraduría Federal del Consumidor, is the federal agency that protects consumers. It was created by the Ley Federal de Protección al Consumidor (1975) and began work in 1976. Some of your rights under that law: a guarantee (garantía) on goods sold with one can't be shorter than 90 days from delivery (the minimum was 60 days until a 2018 reform), and the warranty card (póliza) must say what it covers and how to use it. If a product is defective or isn't what was offered, you can generally ask for a replacement or your money back.

What the law covers is defects, not changing your mind: whether a shop takes back an item that works fine depends on the shop's own policy, so read the notice by the cash register and keep your ticket. If a shop refuses to honor a guarantee, you can call PROFECO's Teléfono del Consumidor (55 5568 8722 in Mexico City, 800 468 8722 from the rest of the country) or file a complaint online. Details change, so check PROFECO's website.

Elsewhere: Words for "broken" vary: in Spain a machine is estropeado or averiado and people say no funciona rather than no sirve; in Peru it's malogrado. And in Chile and Peru a receipt is usually la boleta.

Sources

  • Ley Federal de Protección al Consumidor (Cámara de Diputados, current text): art. 77 (guarantee not less than 90 days), art. 78 (content of the warranty card), art. 92 (replacement or refund for goods that fail to meet what was offered).
  • Reform of the Ley Federal de Protección al Consumidor published in the Diario Oficial de la Federación in 2018: the minimum guarantee rose from 60 to 90 days.
  • PROFECO, Teléfono del Consumidor and history of the institution (gob.mx/profeco; checked October 2026).

Role play: Return a broken item

Last Saturday you bought an electric fan at an electronics shop. It worked on Sunday, but yesterday it stopped turning on. You have the receipt, but you forgot the box. Go back to the shop and get it exchanged, or get your money back. Use usted.

You

  1. Greet the assistant and say what you bought and when.
  2. Explain the problem in the preterite (funcionó… ya no).
  3. Answer the assistant's questions (did you drop it? do you have the receipt?).
  4. Mention what you forgot (se me olvidó…).
  5. Ask for an exchange or a refund, and thank them.

El dependiente

  1. Asks: ¿En qué le puedo ayudar?
  2. Asks what's wrong and if you dropped it.
  3. Asks for the receipt.
  4. Offers an exchange or a refund.
  5. Mentions the guarantee.
