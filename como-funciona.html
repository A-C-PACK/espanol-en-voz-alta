Cómo funciona

How the course works, and how to get the most out of it.

Where to start

If you are new to Spanish, start with Lesson 1.1 and go in order: every lesson builds on the ones before it. If you already know some Spanish, try the unit tests: if you score 70% or more, move on.

The levels

The course follows the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR) and the Instituto Cervantes Plan curricular. A1 is survival Spanish about yourself and your immediate world; A2 is everyday life in the city; B1 is handling the unexpected and saying what you think, with reasons. Each level ends with a review unit and an exam in the style of the official DELE exams.

Every lesson has eight steps

  1. Escucha (listen): a conversation, voiced by Mexican speakers. You can slow it down and show the text and translation.
  2. Fíjate (notice): how the language in the conversation works, explained briefly.
  3. Practica (practice): exercises that start guided and end with your own sentences. Every answer is explained.
  4. Palabras (words): the lesson's vocabulary, voiced. It goes into your Repaso deck.
  5. Lee (read): a short real-world text: a message, a menu, a form, a blog post.
  6. Cultura (culture): something specific about life in Mexico and the Spanish-speaking world.
  7. Tu turno (your turn): a role play and a short piece of writing, with a model and a checklist.
  8. ¿Lo logré? (did I do it?): check yourself against the lesson's goal.

Each lesson shows how long it takes before you start: about an hour. It splits well into two sittings (steps 1–4, then 5–8), and you can stop at any step and continue later from the same place.

Speaking practice

Three ways, use whichever you like: say it aloud (in Chrome, Edge and Safari the course can listen and check; elsewhere you type or reveal the answer), record yourself and compare with the model, and practice with an AI partner: copy the prompt into ChatGPT or Claude, switch to voice mode, and play the scene.

Repaso

Words and phrases you meet come back in Repaso just before you are likely to forget them (after 1, 3, 7 and 16 days). Anything you miss in a test, or mark as todavía no, comes back sooner. Ten minutes a day is enough. The Andamio page drills the 21 "scaffold" structures (tener que, ir a, me gustaría…) that let you say a lot with a few verbs.

Which Spanish?

The Spanish of Mexico City: ustedes for "you all", Mexican words such as camión (bus) and celular (cell phone), and Mexican pronunciation. Culture notes show how other countries differ, and say so clearly when something is from Spain.

Your progress

Progress is saved in this browser. To move to another device, or keep a backup, use Export on Mi progreso, then Import on the other device.

Honest expectations

Working through the whole course takes about 50–60 hours of lessons and tests, plus ten minutes of Repaso a day: roughly six to eight months at three lessons a week. That is less than a classroom course to B1 (often 180–350 hours), so it will get you to B1 tasks; real fluency comes from using Spanish with people, which every lesson prepares you for.
